Wave vectors and bands

One point per line: a label, then two numbers (fractions such as 2/3 are fine). The bands are computed along straight lines from point to point. With π/a units, a = |a₁|.

The cell’s symmetry is checked automatically: when a resonator or a shape breaks the six- or four-fold symmetry, the standard path is replaced by the longer path that symmetry needs (half zone for an asymmetric cell).

Unit cell editor

Build the cell from shapes and parts in top view. The Shape tool places a regular polygon (any number of sides), rectangle or circle, used as a shell plate, a frame of beams, shell walls or a hole: type its centre, size and rotation, or drag it and its handles with the mouse. Or start from a template and edit it. Beams have a width and a height; shell walls are vertical strips with a thickness and a height; shell plates have a thickness and may have holes. Parts may cross the dashed cell outline: everything repeats periodically and points snap to the periodic copies. Mark each part as host or resonator. Run the analysis from the top bar; results appear in the Dispersion tab.

Mass and stiffness sensitivity

Scales one quantity at a time around the current design and recomputes the complete gaps. The table gives log-sensitivities S = ∂ ln f / ∂ ln p at the nominal design: S = −0.5 means a 10 % increase in the parameter lowers that gap edge by about 5 %.

Run a sweep to see the gap map and sensitivities.

Edge waves

A strip N cells wide, repeating along the edge and free on both long sides, is solved for its own bands. Grey areas are the bulk bands projected onto the edge direction. A strip mode outside them that keeps most of its energy in the outer cells is an edge wave; one inside a complete gap is an edge wave the bulk cannot carry.
